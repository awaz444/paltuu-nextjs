The **best cat food in Pakistan** for most owners comes from one of five brands: **Royal Canin** (premium, best for Persians and kittens), **Josera** (premium quality at a mid-range price), **Reflex** (the best value for everyday feeding), **Me-O** (budget, sold in almost every supermarket) and **Whiskas** (budget, best known for its wet pouches). All five are easy to find in Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad, and each one has a kitten formula.

Below you'll find what each brand makes, what it costs in rupees right now, who it suits, and how to avoid the fake and expired bags that turn up in local markets.

> **Prices checked on 8 November 2026** at Pakistani online pet stores and supermarkets, including PetsOne, Petshub, Shaffan Pets, Al-Fatah, Naheed and Springs. Imported pet food prices move with the rupee and shop discounts, so treat these as a guide and check the live price before you buy.

## How We Picked These Brands

We didn't rank on marketing or on who pays us. Nobody paid for a place on this list. A brand made the top five if it:

- Is **actually stocked in Pakistan** by several sellers, not just one importer
- Sells a **complete and balanced** food (it says so on the bag), with real meat listed first or near the top
- Has a **kitten** formula, because most new cat owners on Paltuu start with a kitten
- Covers a clear price point, so there is an honest option at every budget

We also looked at which brands Paltuu's cat owners mention most when they talk about feeding, especially Persian owners in Karachi.

## 1. Royal Canin: Best Premium Cat Food

Royal Canin is widely recommended by vets in Pakistan and is a common choice among Persian breeders. It is a French company owned by Mars, and its range is split by age, breed and health need rather than by flavour.

**What they make:**

- Dry food for **kittens** (Kitten, Mother & Babycat for weaning), **adults** (Fit 32, Indoor, Hairball Care) and **breeds** (Persian, with a kibble shape made for flat faces)
- Wet pouches (85 g) in gravy and jelly
- Veterinary diets for urinary, kidney and digestive problems, which you should only use on a vet's advice

**Price (PKR):** about Rs 2,400–2,900 for a 400 g bag and Rs 9,100–10,600 for 2 kg. Veterinary formulas cost Rs 10,000 or more for 1.5–2 kg. Wet pouches are about Rs 490–530 each.

**Pros:** precise formulas for each life stage, the Persian kibble really does help flat-faced cats eat, and it is consistent from bag to bag.

**Cons:** the most expensive food on this list, and because it is the most popular premium brand it is also the most often faked (see the buying guide below).

## 2. Josera: Best Value Premium Food

Josera is a German family-owned pet food maker now stocked by several Pakistani online pet stores. Its food is close to Royal Canin in quality but costs roughly half as much per kilo.

**What they make:**

- **Josera Kitten** for growing kittens and nursing mothers
- Adult recipes such as Catelux (with duck, good for hairballs), Culinesse (salmon) and Marinesse (fish, grain-free)
- **JosiCat**, a lower-priced line for everyday feeding
- "Help" veterinary diets for kidney, urinary and digestive care, plus wet pouches

**Price (PKR):** about Rs 5,200–5,500 for a 2 kg bag of Kitten, Catelux or Culinesse. Help vet diets are Rs 6,500–7,000 for 2 kg. Wet pouches are about Rs 580–670.

**Pros:** high meat content, no artificial colours, and good value for a European premium brand.

**Cons:** fewer sellers than Royal Canin, so specific recipes can go out of stock, especially outside Karachi and Lahore.

## 3. Reflex: Best Mid-Range Everyday Food

Reflex is a Turkish brand and probably the most common mid-range cat food in Pakistani pet shops. It sits in the sweet spot between budget supermarket food and premium imports.

**What they make:**

- **Reflex** (standard line): adult chicken, gourmet chicken and a kitten formula
- **Reflex Plus**: a step-up line with adult, urinary, sterilised and hairball recipes

**Price (PKR):** about Rs 3,200–4,100 for a 1.5 kg bag of Reflex Plus, and Rs 3,650–4,200 for a 2 kg bag of standard Reflex or Reflex Kitten. Large 15 kg sacks bring the per-kilo cost down for multi-cat homes.

**Pros:** good ingredients for the price, widely stocked, and the Plus Urinary recipe is an affordable option for male cats prone to urinary problems.

**Cons:** less specialised than the premium brands, and some cats get bored of the limited flavours.

## 4. Me-O: Best Budget Food You Can Find Anywhere

Me-O is a Thai brand and the cat food you are most likely to see in a regular supermarket, from Al-Fatah and Springs in Lahore to Naheed and Imtiaz in Karachi. That availability is its biggest strength.

**What they make:**

- Dry adult food in many flavours: tuna, seafood, chicken and vegetable, beef and vegetable, mackerel
- **Me-O Kitten** dry food
- Wet pouches and cans, and a Persian dry formula

**Price (PKR):** about Rs 2,400–3,250 for a 1.2 kg bag, depending on the flavour and the store. Larger 3 kg and 7 kg bags are cheaper per kilo.

**Pros:** cheap, easy to buy on the way home, and the flavour variety helps with picky eaters.

**Cons:** lower meat content than the brands above, and the strong flavours can make some cats reluctant to switch to anything else later.

## 5. Whiskas: Best Budget Wet Food

Whiskas is another Mars brand, and in Pakistan it is best known for its 85 g wet pouches. Dry Whiskas is often out of stock, but the pouches are almost always on the shelf.

**What they make:**

- Wet pouches in jelly and gravy (tuna, chicken, poultry, lamb, beef), plus **kitten** pouches
- Dry adult food (1.2 kg) and **Whiskas Junior** dry food for kittens

**Price (PKR):** wet pouches are about Rs 220–340 each (kitten pouches at the top of that range). Dry 1.2 kg bags run about Rs 2,200–2,950 when available.

**Pros:** cheap, cats love the taste, and a pouch mixed into dry food is an easy way to get more water into your cat during a Karachi summer.

**Cons:** dry food availability is patchy, and a pouch-only diet costs more per day than good dry food.

## Buying Guide: Choosing the Right Food

### Kitten or adult food?

Feed **kitten food until about 12 months** of age. Kittens need more protein, fat and calories than adults, and adult food can leave them short. Every brand above has a kitten formula. For kittens under eight weeks, use a weaning formula such as Royal Canin Mother & Babycat, and never give cow's milk.

### Persian and other long-haired cats

Persians are the most popular breed on Paltuu, and their flat faces make some kibble hard to pick up. Royal Canin Persian and Me-O Persian use shapes made for them. Long-haired cats also swallow more fur, so a hairball formula helps, along with regular brushing (see our [cat grooming guide](/blogs/cat-grooming-basics)).

### Indoor, neutered and male cats

Neutered and indoor cats burn fewer calories, so a "sterilised" or "indoor" recipe helps prevent weight gain. Male cats are more prone to urinary blockages, especially in hot cities where they drink too little. Wet food and a urinary formula both help, but if your cat strains in the litter box, go to a vet the same day. You can find one through [Paltuu's vet listings](/pet-care).

### What to look for on the label

- A named meat (chicken, fish, duck) as the first or second ingredient
- The words **"complete"** or **"complete and balanced"**, not "complementary" (that means it's a treat or topper)
- **Taurine** in the ingredients or nutrition panel
- A clear **expiry date and batch number**

## How to Avoid Fake or Expired Cat Food

Imported cat food is expensive, which makes it a target for fakes and for old stock being resold. To protect your cat:

- **Buy from established sellers**, either known pet shops, large supermarkets or online stores with a real address and return policy. Be careful with very cheap listings on social media.
- **Check the expiry date and batch code** on the bag itself, not on a sticker someone has added. Both should be printed cleanly.
- **Look for the importer's sticker.** Genuine imported bags in Pakistan carry a sticker with the importer's name and address.
- **Inspect the bag.** Blurry printing, spelling mistakes, a resealed top or a different weight than usual are warning signs.
- **Avoid loose kibble sold by weight.** It's often repacked from old or damp stock and can grow mould during the monsoon.
- **If the price is far below every other shop**, assume something is wrong.
- **Store it well.** Keep the bag sealed and in an airtight container. Karachi's humidity can turn an open bag stale in a couple of weeks.

When switching brands, mix the new food in gradually over 7–10 days to avoid stomach upsets. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which is the best cat food in Pakistan?

For most cats, Royal Canin is the best premium choice and Reflex is the best value. If you want premium quality for less, Josera is a strong middle option. Choose based on your cat's age, breed and budget rather than the brand name alone.

### What is the price of Royal Canin cat food in Pakistan?

As of November 2026, a 400 g bag costs about Rs 2,400–2,900 and a 2 kg bag about Rs 9,100–10,600, depending on the formula and the shop. Veterinary diets cost Rs 10,000 or more for 1.5–2 kg.

### What is the best cheap cat food in Pakistan?

Reflex and Me-O are the best budget-friendly dry foods, at roughly Rs 1,800–2,700 per kilo. Whiskas pouches are the cheapest decent wet food at about Rs 220–340 each.

### What should I feed a Persian kitten?

A kitten formula until 12 months, ideally one made for flat-faced breeds such as Royal Canin Persian Kitten. Feed three to four small meals a day and always keep fresh water available.

### Can I feed my cat only wet food?

Yes, if it's labelled "complete", but it costs more per day than dry food. Many owners in Pakistan feed dry food and add one wet pouch a day for extra moisture and taste.
