Winters in Pakistan swing from the biting cold of Murree, Islamabad, and Gilgit-Baltistan to the milder but still chilly nights of Karachi and Lahore. Wherever you are, dropping temperatures can affect your pet's comfort and health more than most owners expect.

This guide covers how to keep dogs and cats warm, spot the early signs of cold-weather stress, and avoid the most common winter health risks for pets in Pakistan.

## Why Winter Care Matters, Even in Milder Cities

Not every pet needs a jacket, but every pet is affected by winter in some way.

- Northern areas and hill stations can drop below freezing, putting outdoor and short-haired pets at real risk of hypothermia.
- Even in Karachi or Lahore, overnight temperatures can fall enough to stress puppies, kittens, senior pets, and small breeds.
- Tiled and marble floors, common in Pakistani homes, get noticeably cold and can affect pets that nap on the ground all day.
- Shorter days often mean less exercise and sunlight, which can affect a pet's mood and joint health.

Cold sensitivity depends heavily on breed, age, coat type, and health status, so the same winter night can be harmless for one pet and risky for another.

## Keeping Dogs Warm and Healthy

### Signs Your Dog Is Cold

- Shivering or trembling
- Curling into a tight ball to conserve heat
- Reluctance to go outside or shortened walks
- Holding up paws or walking gingerly on cold surfaces
- Seeking out blankets, sunny spots, or warm laps

### Dogs That Need Extra Protection

- Short-haired and single-coated breeds (Pugs, Dobermans, Greyhounds, short-haired mixed breeds)
- Small and toy breeds, which lose body heat faster
- Puppies, whose temperature regulation isn't fully developed
- Senior dogs, especially those with arthritis, which often worsens in cold weather
- Dogs that are underweight or have short fur due to recent grooming

> **Pro Tip:** If your dog is shivering on a walk, that's not stubbornness, it's a clear sign to head home and warm up.

## Keeping Cats Warm and Safe

### Indoor Warmth Tips

- Provide a raised bed or blanket away from cold tile or marble floors
- Keep at least one warm, draft-free resting spot away from windows and doors
- Cats often seek out warm appliances, so keep an eye on space heaters and stovetops for safety

### The Car Engine Danger

In colder cities, outdoor and stray cats often climb into car engine bays for warmth overnight. This is one of the most overlooked winter hazards for cats.

> **Important:** Before starting your car on cold mornings, bang on the hood or honk the horn once. It only takes a few seconds and can save a cat's life.

### Outdoor and Community Cats

If you feed community cats near your home, consider setting up a simple insulated shelter (a box lined with straw or blankets, raised off the ground) in a sheltered corner. Straw insulates far better than blankets, which can hold moisture and freeze.

## Winter Grooming Adjustments

Grooming routines should shift slightly in winter rather than stopping altogether.

- Avoid over-bathing. Frequent baths strip natural oils and dry out skin, which is already drier in winter air.
- Dry your pet thoroughly after any bath, especially around the paws, belly, and ears, before letting them outside.
- Keep brushing regularly. A well-maintained coat traps warm air better than a matted one.
- Check paw pads after walks for cracking, dryness, or salt/chemical residue from de-icing products used in colder areas.
- Trim excess hair between paw pads carefully, since ice and mud tend to cling there and cause discomfort.

## Nutrition Changes for Winter

- Dogs that spend a lot of time outdoors in cold weather may need slightly more calories to maintain body heat, similar to how humans eat more in winter.
- Indoor pets with reduced activity may need the opposite: watch portion sizes so they don't gain excess weight.
- Always provide fresh water. Pets can become dehydrated in winter just as easily as summer, and cold water intake sometimes drops without owners noticing.
- Senior pets with joint issues may benefit from vet-recommended joint supplements, which are often more relevant as cold weather aggravates stiffness.

## Common Winter Health Risks

### Hypothermia

A dangerously low body temperature, most common in small, short-haired, senior, or unwell pets left outside too long in cold or wet conditions.

Warning signs include intense shivering, weakness, stiff muscles, and lethargy. Left untreated, it can become life-threatening.

### Frostbite

Affects the ears, tail, and paws first, since these areas have less insulation and blood flow. Look for pale, grey, or bluish skin, and unusual coldness or brittleness in these areas.

### Arthritis Flare-Ups

Cold weather commonly worsens joint pain in older dogs and cats. Watch for stiffness after resting, reluctance to jump or climb stairs, and slower movement in the morning.

### Dry, Cracked Skin and Paws

Cold air and indoor heaters both reduce humidity, which can lead to flaky skin, dandruff, and cracked paw pads.

### Respiratory Sensitivity

Pets with existing respiratory conditions can be more sensitive to cold air, especially during early morning walks.

## Practical Winter Care Checklist

1. Provide a warm, insulated, and draft-free sleeping area off cold floors.
2. Shorten outdoor time during the coldest parts of the day rather than skipping walks entirely.
3. Check paws for cracking or irritation after every walk.
4. Keep a consistent, slightly adjusted feeding routine based on activity level.
5. Bang on your car hood before starting the engine in colder areas.
6. Set up a simple insulated shelter if you care for community cats.
7. Watch senior pets closely for signs of joint stiffness.
8. Keep grooming consistent, but avoid over-bathing.

> **Pro Tip:** A simple, well-fitted sweater can genuinely help short-haired, small, senior, or underweight dogs during walks in colder cities, it isn't just for appearances.

## When to See a Vet

Some winter symptoms need more than home care. See a vet promptly if your pet shows:

- Intense, prolonged shivering or muscle stiffness
- Pale or bluish skin on the ears, paws, or tail
- Sudden reluctance to move, stand, or climb stairs
- Cracked, bleeding, or infected-looking paw pads
- Lethargy, weakness, or reduced appetite during cold snaps

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do dogs in Pakistan actually need sweaters?

Short-haired, small, senior, or underweight dogs in colder cities and hill areas genuinely benefit from a sweater during walks and early mornings. Thick-coated breeds usually don't need one.

### Can cats get hypothermia in Pakistan's winters?

Yes, especially outdoor, stray, or short-haired cats in colder regions. Providing a warm, insulated shelter significantly reduces this risk.

### Should I bathe my pet less often in winter?

Generally yes. Over-bathing dries out skin that's already affected by cold, dry air. Stick to a lighter grooming schedule and dry your pet thoroughly afterward.

### Why do stray cats climb into car engines?

Engine bays retain heat after a car is switched off, making them an attractive (but dangerous) shelter spot for cats on cold nights.

### Does cold weather really affect arthritis in pets?

Yes, vets commonly see increased joint stiffness and reduced mobility in older pets during colder months, similar to what many humans experience.
