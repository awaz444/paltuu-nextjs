## What Should I Feed My Dog?

The short answer is that dogs need a balanced diet built around high-quality animal protein, along with fats, carbohydrates, vitamins, and minerals.

Unlike cats, dogs are omnivores, so their diet can include a wider mix of meat, grains, and vegetables. Still, protein should always be the foundation.

A healthy dog diet should include:

- High-quality protein (chicken, beef, lamb, fish)
- Healthy fats for energy and coat health
- Digestible carbohydrates (rice, oats)
- Vitamins and minerals
- Fresh water at all times

If you're asking **what should I feed my dog**, the safest starting point is a complete commercial dog food formulated for your dog's age and size.

A balanced diet supports:

- Strong muscles and joints
- Healthy skin and coat
- Good digestion
- A strong immune system
- Steady energy levels

Following a proper **dog nutrition guide** is one of the simplest ways to prevent expensive health problems later on.

## Why Dog Nutrition Matters

Poor nutrition is one of the leading causes of preventable health issues in pet dogs.

Feeding low-quality or inconsistent food can lead to:

- Obesity
- Joint problems
- Digestive upset
- Dull coat and excessive shedding
- Nutrient deficiencies
- Low energy and poor immunity

Good nutrition reduces long-term vet visits and helps your dog live a longer, more active life.

When figuring out **what should I feed my dog**, always prioritize ingredient quality over price alone.

### Dry Dog Food (Kibble)

Dry food is the most common choice for Pakistani households.

Benefits include:

- More affordable per meal
- Long shelf life in Pakistan's climate
- Easy to store and portion
- Helps reduce plaque buildup

### Wet Dog Food

Wet or canned food is often used to boost hydration or tempt picky eaters.

Benefits include:

- Higher moisture content
- Easier to chew for puppies and senior dogs
- Stronger aroma and flavor

Many owners mix a small amount of wet food into dry kibble for variety.

## Feeding a Dog in Pakistan

Pakistan's climate and lifestyle can influence how and what you feed your dog.

During peak summer heat, hydration and lighter meals matter even more (see our [summer pet care tips](/blogs/essential-pet-care-tips-summer)).

### Healthy Foods Dogs Can Eat

Dogs can safely enjoy in moderation:

- Cooked chicken (boneless, unseasoned)
- Cooked lean beef
- Plain boiled rice
- Cooked eggs
- Plain yogurt
- Carrots and pumpkin

These can be used as occasional additions to a balanced commercial diet, not a full replacement.

### Foods Dogs Should Never Eat

Some common household foods are toxic to dogs.

Avoid:

- Chocolate
- Onions and garlic
- Grapes and raisins
- Xylitol (found in sugar-free gum and sweets)
- Cooked bones (can splinter)
- Alcohol and caffeine

A responsible **dog nutrition guide** always includes knowing what to keep off the menu.

## Puppy Feeding Guide

Puppies grow fast and need more calories, protein, and fat per kilogram of body weight than adult dogs.

### Birth to 8 Weeks

Puppies should receive:

- Mother's milk
- Vet-approved puppy milk replacer if needed

### 2 to 6 Months

A critical growth window.

Feed:

- High-quality puppy food
- 3–4 small meals daily
- Fresh water at all times

### 6 to 12 Months

Gradually transition towards adult portions, continuing puppy-specific food until your vet recommends switching, usually around 12 months (later for large breeds).

A solid **puppy feeding guide** supports healthy bone growth, brain development, and a strong immune system. For more on this stage, check our guide on [puppy nutrition](/blogs/best-nutrition-for-growing-puppies).

> **Important:** Avoid feeding cow's milk to puppies, as it can cause diarrhea in dogs that are lactose sensitive.

## Adult Dog Feeding Guide

Adult dogs generally do best on a consistent, maintenance-level diet.

Most adult dogs thrive with:

- Two meals daily
- Measured portions based on weight and activity
- A fixed feeding schedule

When answering **what should I feed my dog**, remember that portion control matters as much as ingredient quality — overfeeding is one of the most common mistakes owners make.

## Senior Dog Nutrition

Older dogs often need diet adjustments to match a slower metabolism and changing joint health.

Senior dogs may benefit from:

- Lower-calorie, easily digestible food
- Joint-support supplements (glucosamine, omega-3s)
- Smaller, more frequent meals
- Vet-recommended senior formulas

Regular checkups become more important with age. Learn what a routine exam involves in our guide on [understanding vet visits](/blogs/understanding-vet-visits).

## Dog Feeding Schedule

Another common follow-up question after **what should I feed my dog** is "how often should I feed my dog?"

### Puppies

- 3–4 meals daily

### Adult Dogs

- 2 meals daily

### Senior Dogs

- 2–3 smaller meals daily

A consistent **dog feeding schedule** supports better digestion and helps prevent begging and overeating.

## Signs Your Dog Has a Poor Diet

Nutritional problems usually show up in visible ways.

Watch for:

- Dull or flaky coat
- Sudden weight gain or loss
- Low energy or lethargy
- Frequent digestive upset
- Excessive shedding
- Poor muscle tone

If these signs continue, it's best to consult professional [veterinarians on Paltuu](/pet-care).

## Common Dog Feeding Mistakes

### Overfeeding and Too Many Treats

Treats add up quickly and are a leading cause of canine obesity.

### Feeding Table Scraps

Seasoned or fatty human food can trigger digestive issues and, in some cases, pancreatitis.

### Skipping Water Access

Dogs need constant access to fresh water, especially in Pakistan's hot months.

### Choosing Food by Price Alone

Very cheap dog food often relies on fillers instead of real nutrition.

Choosing **healthy dog food** based on ingredients, not just cost, pays off in fewer vet visits over your dog's lifetime.

## Practical Tips for Better Dog Nutrition

### Actionable Steps

1. Choose food suited to your dog's age and breed size.
2. Measure portions instead of free-feeding.
3. Keep a consistent daily feeding schedule.
4. Always provide fresh water.
5. Introduce new foods gradually.
6. Avoid feeding directly from your plate.
7. Track your dog's weight regularly.
8. Schedule routine veterinary checkups.

> **Pro Tip:** Check your dog's body condition monthly — you should be able to feel (but not see) their ribs.
