The best **food for street cats** in Pakistan is, in order: dry cat food (kibble), plain boiled chicken, cooked eggs, wet cat food pouches, and plain boiled fish, always served with a bowl of clean water. Every one of these is easy to find in Karachi, Lahore or Islamabad, safe for a cat's stomach, and far better than the *doodh-roti* and leftover *salan* most street cats survive on.

Every *mohalla* in Pakistan has its regulars: the tabby waiting outside the bakery, the mother cat raising kittens under a parked car, the colony living behind the *dhaba*. If you've started putting food out for them, this guide covers what to feed, what to avoid, and how to do it without causing problems for the cats or your neighbours.

## Why Street Cats Need the Right Food

Cats are obligate carnivores. They need animal protein and an amino acid called **taurine**, and they can't get it from bread, rice or *daal*. A street cat living on scraps fills its stomach but slowly runs short of nutrients, which shows up as:

- A dull, patchy coat and visible ribs
- Weak immunity, so more eye infections and skin problems
- Kittens that fail to thrive or die in their first weeks
- Higher vulnerability to heatstroke in summer and illness in winter

You don't need an expensive diet to fix this. A small amount of the *right* food does more good than a large amount of the wrong food.

## The Top 5 Foods for Street Cats

### 1. Dry Cat Food (Kibble): The Best All-Rounder

If you only pick one food, make it a complete dry cat food. It's designed to give a cat everything it needs, including taurine, in a single handful.

**Why it works for street feeding:**

- It doesn't spoil in Pakistan's 40°C+ summers the way meat does
- You can keep a bag in your car or bike for the cats you meet on your route
- Portioning is easy: roughly a small cup (40–60 g) per adult cat per day
- It costs less per meal than fresh chicken, especially if you're feeding several cats

Budget brands such as **Whiskas**, **Me-O** and **Reflex** are widely available at pet shops and larger supermarkets. Premium brands like **Royal Canin** and **Brit Care** are excellent but usually far more than a stray-feeding budget needs. Buying the larger bag (3 kg and up) brings the per-meal cost down significantly. You can compare options and prices on [Paltuu Bazaar](/bazaar).

> **Tip:** Avoid buying loose, unbranded kibble sold by weight. It's often stale or stored in damp conditions and can grow mould during the monsoon.

### 2. Plain Boiled Chicken: The Best Fresh Food

Chicken is the most cat-friendly fresh protein available in every Pakistani city. Boil it in plain water, with **no salt, no masala, no oil, no onion and no garlic**, then shred it and let it cool.

**How to keep it affordable:**

- Ask your local *murghi wala* for chicken necks, gizzards (*pota*) and offcuts. They're much cheaper than boneless meat and cats love them.
- Liver (*kaleji*) is very nutritious but should be no more than one small portion a week. Too much liver causes vitamin A toxicity.
- The leftover boiling water (unsalted) makes a good broth to pour over kibble on hot days for extra hydration.

> **Important:** Always remove cooked bones. Cooked chicken bones splinter and can choke a cat or pierce its gut. Raw meat carries a higher risk of salmonella and parasites, so cook it for street cats.

### 3. Cooked Eggs: Cheap, Quick Protein

An egg is one of the cheapest complete proteins you can buy, and most cats eat it readily. Serve it **hard-boiled and mashed** or **scrambled in a dry pan** with no butter, oil or salt.

- One egg is a good meal supplement for one or two adult cats
- Mash it into kibble to make it more appealing to a sick or picky cat
- Eggs are particularly good for nursing mother cats, who need extra protein

**Never feed raw egg.** Raw egg white contains avidin, which blocks the absorption of biotin (a B-vitamin), and raw eggs can carry salmonella.

### 4. Wet Cat Food Pouches: Best for Kittens, Seniors and Sick Cats

Wet food costs more per meal than kibble, but it has two big advantages: it's around 75–80% water, and it's soft. That makes it the right choice for:

- **Kittens** that are just starting on solid food (around 4 weeks and older)
- **Elderly cats** with missing or worn teeth
- **Injured or sick cats** that won't eat dry food
- **Peak summer** in Karachi, Hyderabad or Multan, when dehydration is a real danger

Keep a few pouches at home for emergencies rather than using them as the everyday food. Once opened, wet food should be eaten within 30–60 minutes in hot weather. Pick up leftovers so they don't rot or attract flies.

### 5. Plain Boiled Fish: A Treat, Not a Staple

Cats love fish, and in coastal Karachi fish offcuts are often easy to get. Boil it plain, **remove every bone**, and serve in small amounts. Canned tuna is fine occasionally, but only the kind packed **in water**, never in oil or brine.

Keep fish to **two or three times a week**:

- Raw fish contains thiaminase, an enzyme that destroys vitamin B1
- An all-fish diet can lead to vitamin E deficiency and urinary problems
- Fish smells strong and attracts crows and other animals to your feeding spot

## Don't Forget Water: The Most Important "Food"

Across most of Pakistan, a street cat is more likely to suffer from dehydration than from hunger. Open drains and puddles are often contaminated, and in May–June there may be no water at all.

- Put out a wide, shallow bowl of clean water and refill it daily
- A clay *mitti ka bartan* keeps water noticeably cooler in summer
- Place it in the shade, away from direct afternoon sun

## Foods You Should Never Give Street Cats

Many common, well-meant Pakistani feeding habits actually make cats sick. Avoid:

| Food | Why it's harmful |
|---|---|
| **Cow or buffalo milk (*doodh*)** | Most adult cats are lactose intolerant, so milk causes diarrhoea and dehydration |
| **Leftover salan, biryani or karahi** | Onion, garlic, chillies and salt damage red blood cells and upset the gut |
| **Roti, bread or plain rice alone** | Fills the stomach with no meaningful nutrition for a carnivore |
| **Cooked bones** | Splinter and cause choking or internal injury |
| **Chocolate, tea, coffee** | Contain theobromine or caffeine, which are toxic to cats |
| **Grapes and raisins** | Linked to kidney damage in pets |
| **Dog food as a main diet** | Lacks the taurine and protein levels cats need |
| **Raw Eid meat scraps** | Risk of parasites and bacteria. Cook any meat you share after Eid ul Adha. |

## How to Feed Street Cats Responsibly

Feeding is kind, but feeding badly can create conflict with neighbours and put the cats at risk. A few habits make a big difference:

![A small colony of street cats gathered on concrete steps, one of them eating](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1785996757953-7a2b78ba65fc?auto=format&fit=crop&q=80&w=1200)

**Pick a fixed time and place.** Cats learn routines quickly. Feeding at the same spot once or twice a day keeps the colony from wandering into roads or homes looking for food.

**Feed away from traffic and doorways.** A quiet corner, an empty plot or the back of a building is better than the main gate or the street.

**Clean up after every meal.** Leftover food attracts rats, crows and flies, and it is the number-one reason neighbours turn against feeders. Use a plate or a bowl, never paper, and take it away afterwards.

**Don't overfeed.** Put out roughly what the cats will finish in 20–30 minutes. Uneaten food in the heat is a health hazard.

**Watch for health problems.** Feeding time is when you'll notice limping, eye discharge, weight loss or a mother cat with kittens. Fleas and ticks are common on strays. Our [flea and tick prevention guide](/blogs/flea-tick-parasite-prevention-pakistan) explains what to look for. For anything serious, use Paltuu to [find a vet near you](/pet-care); our guide on [finding a vet in Pakistan](/blogs/how-to-find-a-vet-near-you-pakistan) walks you through the options.

**Support spay/neuter (TNR).** A single unspayed female can produce dozens of kittens in her lifetime, most of which won't survive the street. Several rescue groups and clinics in Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad run trap-neuter-return programmes. Ask other feeders in your city on the [Paltuu community feed](/home) which groups are active near you.

**Stay safe.** Don't hand-feed or pick up an unfamiliar cat. If you are scratched or bitten, wash the wound with soap and running water for 15 minutes and see a doctor about rabies vaccination the same day.

## What About Street Kittens?

Very young kittens need special care:

- **Under 4 weeks:** They can't eat solid food. Don't give cow's milk. Use a kitten milk replacer from a pet shop, and get help quickly from a vet or local rescuers. Posting on the [Paltuu community feed](/home) is a fast way to reach them. Orphaned kittens this young rarely survive without help.
- **4–8 weeks:** Wet kitten food or kibble soaked in warm water until soft, 4 small meals a day.
- **8 weeks and up:** Kitten kibble, plus boiled chicken and egg as extras.

Before "rescuing" kittens, watch from a distance for a few hours. The mother is usually nearby, out hunting, and kittens do best with her.

Sometimes the "street cat" turning up at your feeding spot is someone's lost pet. A collar, unusual friendliness or a pedigree breed are clues. Ask around nearby before assuming a cat is a stray. And if you've grown attached to one of your regulars, consider giving them a permanent home, or [browse cats waiting for adoption](/browse-pets) near you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the cheapest healthy food for street cats in Pakistan?

Budget dry cat food bought in a larger bag is usually the cheapest complete diet per meal. Chicken offcuts and necks from your local *murghi wala*, boiled plain, are the cheapest healthy fresh option.

### Can I give milk to street cats?

No. Most adult cats are lactose intolerant, and cow or buffalo milk causes diarrhoea, which can quickly dehydrate a street cat. Give clean water instead. For orphaned kittens, use a kitten milk replacer.

### Can street cats eat roti or rice?

A small amount mixed with chicken won't hurt, but roti and rice on their own provide almost no useful nutrition for cats. They need animal protein to stay healthy.

### How often should I feed street cats?

Once or twice a day at a fixed time and place is ideal. Put out only what the cats will finish in 20 to 30 minutes, and clean up afterwards.

### Is it safe to feed street cats leftover chicken salan?

No. Salan, karahi and biryani contain onion, garlic, salt and spices, which are harmful to cats. If you want to share chicken, set some aside before seasoning and boil it plain.
