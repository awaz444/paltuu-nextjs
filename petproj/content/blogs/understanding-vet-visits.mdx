Regular check-ups are vital for catching problems early, and knowing what actually happens during a standard veterinary examination in Pakistan makes the whole process far less stressful, for you and your pet.

## Physical Exam

Every standard visit starts with a head-to-tail physical exam, usually the first thing that happens once you're in the room. A thorough vet works through the same checklist every time, which is part of what to look for if you're still [choosing a clinic](/blogs/how-to-find-a-vet-near-you-pakistan) — a rushed, five-second glance is a warning sign, not efficiency.

### What the Vet Examines

**Eyes**: Checking for clarity, discharge, or signs of infection that could point to allergies, injury, or a systemic illness.

**Ears**: Looking for redness, odor, mites, or excessive wax, which are especially common in Pakistan's humid coastal cities like Karachi.

**Teeth and Gums**: Assessing dental health, tartar buildup, and gum disease, since dental issues are one of the most under-treated problems in pets here.

**Heart and Lungs**: Listening for irregular heartbeats or breathing issues with a stethoscope, catching problems long before they show visible symptoms.

**Skin and Coat**: Checking for parasites, lumps, or skin conditions, particularly relevant given Pakistan's flea and tick season.

**Abdomen**: Palpating to check organ size and detect abnormalities like bloating, pain, or masses.

## Vaccinations

Keeping vaccinations up to date protects your pet from common and deadly diseases, and it matters more in Pakistan than in many countries given how widespread stray-animal contact is in cities like Karachi and Lahore.

### Core Vaccinations for Dogs

- **Rabies**: Required by law, protects against fatal disease
- **Distemper**: Prevents serious viral infection
- **Parvovirus**: Protects against highly contagious intestinal disease
- **Adenovirus**: Guards against hepatitis and respiratory disease

### Core Vaccinations for Cats

- **Rabies**: Essential protection
- **Feline Distemper (FVRCP)**: Combination vaccine
- **Feline Herpesvirus**: Respiratory protection
- **Calicivirus**: Prevents oral and respiratory disease

## Parasite Prevention

Your vet will discuss:

- **Flea and tick prevention**: Monthly treatments, especially important during Pakistan's warmer months when flea populations spike.
- **Heartworm prevention**: Essential given the mosquito activity across most of Pakistan's climate zones.
- **Deworming**: Regular intestinal parasite control, since stray-animal contact and outdoor exposure raise the risk in most Pakistani cities.

## Weight and Nutrition Assessment

The vet will:

- Weigh your pet and track changes
- Assess body condition score
- Provide dietary recommendations
- Discuss portion control and feeding schedules

## Behavioral Discussion

This is your chance to discuss:

- Any behavioral changes
- Training challenges
- Anxiety or aggression issues
- Changes in appetite or energy levels

## Diagnostic Tests

Depending on age and health, the vet may recommend:

### For Puppies/Kittens

- Fecal examination for parasites
- Blood tests to establish baseline

### For Adult Pets

- Annual blood work
- Urinalysis
- Heartworm testing

### For Senior Pets (7+ years)

- Comprehensive blood panel
- Kidney and liver function tests
- Thyroid screening
- X-rays if needed

## How Often Should You Visit?

**Puppies/Kittens**: Every 3-4 weeks until 16 weeks old

**Adult Pets (1-7 years)**: Annual checkup

**Senior Pets (7+ years)**: Bi-annual checkups

**Chronic Conditions**: As recommended by vet

## Preparing for Your Vet Visit

### Bring These Items

- Previous medical records
- List of current medications
- Questions or concerns written down
- Favorite treats for positive reinforcement
- Leash (dogs) or carrier (cats)

### Information to Share

- Changes in eating or drinking habits
- Bathroom habits
- Energy levels
- Any unusual behaviors
- Recent travels

## Cost Considerations

Typical vet visit costs in Pakistan:

- **Routine checkup**: PKR 1,000-3,000
- **Vaccinations**: PKR 800-2,000 per vaccine
- **Diagnostic tests**: PKR 1,500-8,000
- **Emergency visits**: PKR 3,000+, plus treatment costs

## Emergency Warning Signs

Contact your vet immediately if you notice:

- Difficulty breathing
- Seizures
- Severe vomiting or diarrhea
- Inability to urinate
- Bleeding that won't stop
- Suspected poisoning
- Collapse or unconsciousness

## After the Visit

- Follow all medication instructions carefully
- Schedule follow-up appointments as needed
- Monitor your pet for any adverse reactions
- Keep vaccination records updated

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a standard vet visit take?

A routine checkup typically takes 15-30 minutes. Visits involving diagnostics, vaccinations, or a first-time consultation can run 30-45 minutes, longer if lab results are needed on the spot.

### Do I need to fast my pet before a vet visit?

Only if blood work involving fasting glucose or lipid levels is planned, and your vet will tell you in advance. For a routine checkup or vaccination, normal feeding is fine.

### Can I request a female vet or specific vet by name?

Yes, most clinics in Pakistan will accommodate this if you call ahead, especially larger clinics in Karachi, Lahore, and Islamabad with multiple vets on staff.

### What if my pet is too anxious for a clinic visit?

A [home vet visit](/blogs/home-vet-visits-pakistan) is often a better option for anxious or aggressive pets, since it removes the stressful waiting room and car ride entirely for routine care.

### Is it normal for a vet to recommend tests I didn't ask for?

Yes, if the physical exam reveals something that warrants further investigation. A trustworthy vet will explain why a specific test is recommended and give you a cost estimate before running it, rather than just adding it to the bill.
